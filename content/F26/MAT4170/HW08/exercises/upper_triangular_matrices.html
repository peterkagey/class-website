Recall that an upper triangular matrix in \(\mathbb{R}^{n \times n}\) is a matrix where the entry \(a_{ij} = 0\) whenever \(i > j\).
  1. Use the definition of an upper triangular matrix to prove that the sum of two upper triangular matrices is upper triangular.
  2. Write an argument (as formally or informally as you'd like) that convinces yourself that upper triangular matrices form an abelian group under addition.
  3. Recall the definition of matrix multiplication. If \( A, B \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n} \) with entries \( a_{ij} \) and \( b_{ij} \) respectively then \( AB = C \) has entries \[ c_{ij} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_{ik} b_{kj}. \] Use this definition to prove that if \(A\) and \(B\) are upper triangular matrices then \(C = AB\) is also an upper triangular matrix.
  4. Upper triangular matrices form a ring under matrix addition and matrix multiplication, but when \(n \geq 2\) they do not form a field. Why not?
