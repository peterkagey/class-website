Outline

Also go through homework and rearrange problems.

  1. Intuition for symmetry
    1. Examples of groups.
      1. Early and frequent use of \( GL_n(\mathbb{R}) \) as an example of a group, and discussion of this as a "symmetry."
      2. \( SO_2(\mathbb{R}) \)
      3. \( (\mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\}, \times) \)
    2. Explicit instructions on drawing Cayley Diagrams.
    3. Generators and relations. Particularly \( rf = fr^{-1} \in D_n \).
    4. Isomorphism early: \( C_n \) and \( \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \) along with \( S_3 \) and \( D_3 \).
    5. Abelian groups early.
    6. Direct product of groups.
  2. Discuss conjugation early, along with conjugation as an example of an automorphism.
  3. Equivalence relations.
    1. Cosets (particularly integers mod \( n\))
    2. Conjugacy classes
    3. Orbits of a group action
  4. Isomorphisms and homomorphisms
      Automorphisms and the automorphism group.
