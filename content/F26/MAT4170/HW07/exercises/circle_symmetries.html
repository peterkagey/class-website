1. Do the following exercises about quotienting $(\mathbb{R},+)$.
    1. Prove that the set of rotation matrices \[ SO_2(\mathbb{R}) = \left\{\begin{pmatrix}\cos \theta & -\sin \theta \\ \sin \theta & \cos \theta \end{pmatrix}\middle|\, \theta \in \mathbb{R} \right\} \] forms a group under matrix multiplication.
    2. Prove that the map $\phi \colon \mathbb{R} \to GL_2(\mathbb{R})$ given by \[ \phi(x) = \begin{pmatrix}\cos(2\pi x) & -\sin(2\pi x) \\ \sin(2\pi x) & \cos(2\pi x) \end{pmatrix} \] is a homomorphism but is not injective (1-to-1) or surjective (onto). (Recall that \(GL_2(\mathbb{R})\) is the set of invertible \(2 \times 2\) real-valued matrices.)
    3. Determine the kernel and image of $\phi$.
    4. What group is $\mathbb{R}/\ker(\phi)$ isomorphic to?
  2. Consider the direct product \(G \times H\), and consider the subgroup \[ \mathbf{1} \times H = \{(e_G, h) \mid h \in H\}, \] where \(\mathbf{1}\) denotes the trivial subgroup of \(G\).
    1. Prove that \(\mathbf{1} \times H \triangleleft G \times H\).
    2. Give an example of a homomorphism \(\phi \colon G \times H \to G\) such that \({\ker(\phi) = \mathbf{1} \times H}.\)
    3. Use the first isomorphism theorem to prove that the quotient group \({(G\times H)/(\mathbf{1} \times H)}\) is isomorphic to \(G\).
  3. Use the fact that there is a group homomorphism \(\phi \colon \mathbb{Z} \to C_n\) which sends \(\phi(1) = r\) to prove that \(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \cong C_n\).
