Recall that we say that \(a, a' \in G\) are conjugate if there exists some \(b \in G\) such that \[b * a * b^{-1} = a'.\]

Prove that if \((L, \star)\) and \((H, \bullet)\) are groups, \(\phi\colon L \to H\) is a group homomorphism, and \(g, g' \in L\) are conjugate, then \(\phi(g)\) and \(\phi(g')\) are conjugate in \(H\).
