Suppose that you have three coins, and you can flip over any subset of them and you can permute their places. Some examples of the states of these coins are HTT, THT, HTT and so on. Let's call this group \(W_3\) where the subscript \(3\) stands for the three coins.

We're going to show that this is related to the (Coxeter) group \(B_3\) of symmetries of the cube \[ B_3 = \langle r_1, r_2, r_3 \mid r_1^2 = r_2^2 = r_3^2 = (r_1r_2)^4 = (r_2r_3)^3 = (r_1r_3)^2 = e\rangle, \] where \(B_3\) consists of the \(48\) symmetries of the cube (including reflections).

  1. [3] Show that \(W_3\) can be generated by the following three elements:
    1. \(x\), which is flipping over the left coin,
    2. \(s_1\), which is swapping the left and middle coins, and
    3. \(s_2\), which is swapping the middle and right coins.
  2. [2] Show that there is a homomorphism \(\varphi\colon B_3 \to W_3\) with \(\varphi(r_1) = x\), \(\varphi(r_2) = s_1\), and \(\varphi(r_3) = s_2\).
  3. [1] A flag of a cube is a choice of a face of the cube, followed by an edge on that face, followed by a vertex on that edge (i.e., \(v \subset e \subset f\).) The number of flags of a cube is equal to its number of symmetries. How many flags (and thus symmetries) does a cube have?
  4. [2] Prove that the homomorphism \(\varphi\colon B_3 \to W_3\) is an isomorphism by computing the order of \(W_3\) and using the fact that every generator of \(W_3\) is in the image of the map and thus the map is surjective (onto).

Here's the generalization to the symmetries of the \(n\)-dimensional hypercube: \[ B_n = \langle r_1, r_2, \dots, r_n \mid r_i^2 = (r_1r_2)^4 = (r_jr_{j+1})^3 = (r_kr_\ell)^2 = e, \text{ for } 1 < j < n \text{ and } |k - \ell| \geq 2 \rangle. \] In this case, we're dealing with \(n\) coins, and the homomorphism \(\varphi\colon B_n \to W_n\) sends
    • \(r_1\) to "flip over the leftmost coin" and
    • \(r_k\) to "swap coins \(k-1\) and \(k\)."
