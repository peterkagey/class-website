Exercise 5 [6] (Isomorphism and quotients)
  1. [4] Suppose that \(G = D_{12}\) and \(N = \langle r^3 \rangle\).
    1. Prove that \([G:N] = 6\) and \(N \triangleleft G\) is a normal subgroup.
    2. Make a Cayley diagram or Cayley table for the quotient group \(G/N\), and prove that it is isomorphic to \(S_3\) by giving an explicit isomorphism \({\phi \colon G/N \to S_3}\). (i.e., specify where each coset of \(N\) goes under \(\phi\).)
  2. [2] Prove that if \(G\) is a group, and \(H \leq G\) such that \([G:H] = 2\), then \(H \triangleleft G\) is a normal subgroup, and \(G/H\) is isomorphic to \(C_2\).

Choice Exercise 10 [≤6] (Changing perspective on conjugation)
  1. [3] Conjugating matrices. In linear algebra matrix conjugation (\(BAB^{-1}\)) allows us to "change perspective" by changing basis. That is, if you are thinking of a linear transformation \(A\) with respect to a given basis, and I am thinking about the same linear transformation but in another basis related by \(B\), we can "translate" between our perspectives with (matrix) conjugation: \(A' = BAB^{-1}\).
    Give an example of how this works (perhaps by watching a YouTube video or reading a textbook that discusses this), and write a few sentences explaining what's going on in your own words.
  2. [3] Conjugating permutations. Suppose that we're thinking about $S_n$, and we're scrambling $n$ objects, but the objects are randomly positioned on the table. Reasonable minds could disagree about which position is position $1$ versus position $2$ versus position $n$. You come up with some labeling and I come up with some labeling too, and our labelings are related by the permutation \(\sigma\), which tells that what you are calling position \(1\), I am calling position \(\sigma(1)\).
    Use this way of thinking to find \(\sigma \in S_7\) such that \[ \sigma (314)(25)(67) \sigma^{-1} = (124)(65)(37), \] and explain formally or informally why conjugating permutations preserves cycle structure (e.g., in the above example the conjugates both consist of one \(3\)-cycle and two \(2\)-cycles.)
