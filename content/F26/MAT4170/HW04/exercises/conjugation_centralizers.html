Given \(a,b \in (G,*)\) we say \(a\) is conjugate to \(b\) if there exists some \(g \in G\) such that \(a = g * b * g^{-1}\).

  1. [4] Suppose that we have a square on the ground and you are standing south of it and your friend is standing west of it. You apply \(f \in D_4\) to the square, flipping it horizontally. You ask your friend what symmetry you applied, and your friend replies that you applied \(r^2f \in G\).
    The reason that \(f\) can look like \(r^2f\) from a different perspective is what it means for \(f\) and \(r^2f\) to be conjugate.
    1. Prove \(f\) and \(r^2f\) are conjugate in \(D_4\) by finding an element \(g \in D_4\) such that \(f = g * r^2f * g^{-1}\).
    2. Prove that for all \(g \in D_4\), \(r^2 = g * r^2 * g^{-1}\). It might be useful to use the fact that every element in \(D_4\) can be written as \(r^k\) or \(r^kf\). This means that \(r^2\) is only conjugate to itself.
    3. Determine all elements in \(D_4\) that are conjugate to \(r\).
  2. [3] Prove that "is conjugate to" is an equivalence relation.
  3. [3+1+2] The center of a group \((G,*)\) is denoted \(Z(G)\) and consists of the elements that commute with every element in \(G\) \[ Z(G) = \{ z \in G \mid z = g*z*g^{-1} \forall g \in G \}. \] In terms of conjugation, this can be described as the "subgroup of all elements that are conjugate only to themselves" or the symmetries that look the same from every perspective.
    1. Show that for every group \(G\), the center of the group \(Z(G)\) is a subgroup of \(G\).
    2. Prove that for every group \(G\) the center of the group is an abelian group.
    3. Determine the center of \(D_4\), the dihedral group of the square.
