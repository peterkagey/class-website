In class, we defined an equivalence relation as a relation that is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive—but this isn't a very good definition if I don't tell you what a relation is to begin with!

Formally, a relation \(R\) on a set \(X\) is a (perhaps improper) subset of the Cartesian product of sets \(R \subseteq X \times X\), and we say \(x_1 \mathbin{\mathrm{R}} x_2\) if and only if \((x_1,x_2) \in R\).

  1. [1] Let \(X = \{1, 2, 3\}\), and write out the relation "less than" formally as a subset of \(X \times X\).
  2. [2] Determine if the following relations are reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. Explain why each has or does not have each property.
    1. Let \(X\) be subsets of \(\{1, 2, \dots, 5\}\), and let \(R\) be the relation "is a proper subset of".
    2. Let \(X\) be the set of points in the Cartesian plane \(\mathbb R^2\), and let \(R\) be the relation where \(\vec x_1 \mathbin{\mathrm{R}} \vec x_2\) if and only if \(|\vec x_1 - \vec x_2| \leq 1\).
    3. Let \(X\) be the set of triangles in the plane and let \(R\) be the relation "is similar to".
    4. Let \(X\) be the set of positive integers \(R\) be the relation "is a divisor of".
