1. The quiz from Tuesday of week 3 asked you to prove that \(7\mathbb{Z}\) is a group.
    Answer key.

    We begin by observing that we can write every element of \(7\mathbb{Z}\) in the form \(2k\) where \(k \in \mathbb{Z}\).

    Our binary operation is closed because \[ 7n + 7m = 7(n+m), \] and when \(n, m \in \mathbb{Z}\) so is \(n + m\).

    We know that addition is associative on this set, because addition is associative on the integers, and \(7\mathbb{Z}\) is a subset of the integers. (You can take for granted in this class that both addition and multiplication are associative on the natural, rational, real, and complex numbers.)

    The additive identity is \( 0 = 7(0) \in 7\mathbb{Z} \).

    The inverse of \(7n\) is \(-7n = 7 (-n) \in 7\mathbb{Z},\) because if \(n \in \mathbb{Z}\), then \(-n \in \mathbb{Z}\).
