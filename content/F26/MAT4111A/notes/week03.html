Geometry

Triangle inequality

Suppose that \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) are the side lengths of a triangle, then \(c < a + b\).

The triangle inequality follows directly from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

Hint #1

Start by finding an example that works, even if its not the smallest. One possibility is a triangle whose side lengths belong to a "Pythagorean triple," integers \(a, b, c\) such that \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\).

Hint #2

One possibility is the triangle with a vertex at the origin and other vertices at \((3,0)\) and \((0,4)\). This corresponds to the Pythagorean triple \(3, 4, 5\). Now we need to determine if there are any triangles where one side is length 1 or length 2.

Hint #3

Suppose for the sake of contradiction that \(AB = 1\), we can assume without loss of generality that we can place \(A\) at the origin and \(B\) at \(1,0\). (Why can we assume this?) What does the triangle inequality tell us about the other side lengths, and what does this tell us about \(C\)?

Hint #4

Suppose for the sake of contradiction that \(AB = 2\), we can assume without loss of generality that we can place \(A\) at the origin and \(B\) at \(2,0\). (Why can we assume this?) Where can we place \(C\)?

Heron's formula

Heron's formula states that a triangle with side lengths \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\), and semi-perimeter \((a+b+c)/2\) has area \[ A = \sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)} \]

For $i = 1,2$ let $T_i$ be a triangle with side lengths $a_i, b_i, c_i$, and area $A_i$. Suppose that \[ a_1 \le a_2, b_1 \le b_2, c_1 \le c_2, \] and that $T_2$ is an acute triangle. Does it follow that \(A_1 \le A_2\)?

2004 Putnam, Problem A2
Hint #1

Is the statement true if $T_2$ is not acute? Why not? We can use Heron's formula here, but how does the assumption about \(T_2\) being acute get used?

Hint #2

Assume without loss of generality that \(a_2 \leq b_2 \leq c_2\). A triangle is acute precisely when $a^2 + b^2 \geq c^2$. Therefore the area of \(T_i\) is \( \sqrt{(\frac{a_i + b_i + c_i}{2})(\frac{-a_i + b_i + c_i}{2})(\frac{a_i - b_i + c_i}{2})(\frac{a_i + b_i - c_i}{2})} = \frac{1}{4}\sqrt{4a_i^2b_i^2 - (a_i^2 + b_i^2 - c_i^2)^2} \)

Proof conclusion

Now we observe that since \(a_1^2 + b_2^2 - c_2^2 > 0\) \[ \frac{1}{4}\sqrt{4a_1^2b_1^2 - (a_1^2 + b_1^2 - c_1^2)^2} \leq \frac{1}{4}\sqrt{4a_2^2b_2^2 - (a_1^2 + b_1^2 - c_1^2)^2} \\ \leq \dots \\ \leq \frac{1}{4}\sqrt{4a_i^2b_i^2 - (a_i^2 + b_i^2 - c_i^2)^2} \]
